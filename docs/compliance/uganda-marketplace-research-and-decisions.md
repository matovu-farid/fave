## Purpose and scope

This memo supports Fave issues #6 (driver application and national-ID submission), #7 (admin driver review), #8 (vehicle listings, photos and availability), and #10 (client trip request and vehicle selection). It records primary-source findings, current implementation implications, and questions for qualified Ugandan counsel and the competent authorities.

The codebase already reads versioned policy documents, verification checklists, and retention policies from D1. It blocks sensitive workflows if approved records are missing. This memo and its companion drafts are not approved policy records and must not be published to that registry without counsel approval.

## Research method and source currency

Research used the Uganda Legal Information Institute (ULII), the Ministry of Works and Transport, NITA-U/PDPO, Uganda Revenue Authority, and NIST's official publication catalogue. Consolidated Acts were available through 31 December 2023 for several sources; the VAT Act page warns that outstanding amendments have not been applied. Statutory instruments are linked to their publication. Sources were reviewed 2–3 October 2026, but this is not a complete Gazette search or a formal opinion. Counsel should check later amendments, commencement instruments, licence conditions, administrative guidance, and decisions before relying on it.

## Findings and product implications

### Digital transport network and driver eligibility

The 2020 amendment inserted the transportation-network-company and digital-network driver concepts into the Traffic and Road Safety Act. Section 70A addresses authorization of an online digital platform for public-service transport and an operator's licence for a person wishing to carry passengers or goods for reward through it. The 2023 Regulations prohibit operating the covered platform without a licence (reg. 3), set application and annual compliance requirements (regs. 4–8), and require a licensee to register transport providers and passengers and enter a service agreement with the transport provider (reg. 17).

Regulation 17(2) says the licensee must not grant access to online public-service transport to a person who is not registered with the licensee and licensed with the Competent Authority. Regulation 18 requires suitability assessment/due diligence and a registration system that records full vehicle details and driver/owner details, including a passport photo, a copy of the national ID card, passport number for a foreigner or refugee ID number for a refugee, and the driving-licence number where applicable. It requires provider terms acceptance before platform access, access controls against unregistered providers/passengers, identification of registered people/providers, and privacy consistent with the Data Protection and Privacy Act. Regulation 19 requires a unique provider identifier that is not a phone number or registration plate and secure account activation.

**Product implication:** Fave's first release is limited to Ugandan citizen drivers. It collects a National ID as the driver's identity document, plus the separately required passport-style photo, driving licence details where applicable, vehicle details, and explicit service-agreement acceptance. Foreign-national and refugee driver applications are out of scope and must be clearly declined. The Regulations also describe passport details for foreign nationals and refugee-ID details for refugees; their existence in the law does not mean Fave accepts those categories. Counsel and the Competent Authority must confirm that this restricted product scope and the exact citizen checklist are lawful for Fidexa LLC's service model. An application is not permission to provide rides; do not activate a provider until licensing/registration prerequisites are verified.

**Implementation status and remaining gate:** Fidexa LLC has decided to accept only Ugandan citizen drivers in the first release; foreign nationals and refugees are not eligible. The #6 app now discloses that limit and requires a citizenship affirmation. The API records the affirmation timestamp and blocks application submission or approval without it. The driver evidence flow continues to request only a National ID as the identity document (number and front/back images), plus a separate passport-style photo. The reviewer must still verify the submitted National ID. Counsel and the Competent Authority must confirm that this restriction and the citizen checklist are lawful before activation. Do not represent the product choice as a limitation imposed by the Regulations.

### Vehicle registration, photos, availability, and road use

The 2023 Regulations call for full vehicle details (reg. 18(3)(a)). Regulation 25 requires a transport-provider vehicle mark identifying the licensee and says the mark is subject to Competent Authority approval. The Traffic and Road Safety Act s. 33 addresses vehicle registration, plates and insurance; the Act defines a public service vehicle as a passenger vehicle licensed to carry passengers for hire or reward. The Ministry identifies PSV licensing and vehicle roadworthiness/environmental inspection as functions of the transport-regulation department.

**Product implication:** capture make, model, year, seating, registration and ownership/authorization information, but keep raw registration evidence private. Publish only approved images of the exact vehicle. Checklist fields for PSV/operator permit, inspection/fitness certificate, vehicle registration, insurance type/period/covered use, and approved Fave marking remain subject to counsel/authority confirmation. Availability and prevention of overlapping holds are prudent platform controls derived from the trip product, not a substitute for a statutory certificate.

### Electronic consumer disclosures and trip booking

Electronic Transactions Act s. 23 requires an online supplier to disclose its full name and legal status, physical and electronic contact details, company registration particulars, service characteristics, full price including fees/taxes, payment method, contract terms, service timing, how the transaction record is accessed, return/refund policy, dispute-resolution code if any, and payment/privacy security information. It also requires an opportunity to review the entire transaction, correct errors, and withdraw before placing an order. Failure to comply can create a 14-day cancellation remedy under s. 23(3), subject to exceptions in s. 23(7). Section 23(7)(j) excludes specified-date transport services from that *section 23(3) remedy*. Section 24 contains a separate cancellation provision. Section 27 voids provisions that exclude rights in Part IV.

**Product implication:** the booking confirmation must summarize pickup/drop-off, exact dates, party and luggage details, selected vehicle, provider identity disclosure timing, complete fare and fees, deposit and balance, service window, payment/cancellation process, terms version, and record access before an order is placed. Do not assume the specified-date exception eliminates all cancellation rights or set a cancellation/refund rule until counsel analyzes whether Fave, the driver, or another party is the supplier and how a deposit/hold is structured.

### Personal data, national ID, notices, and retention

The Data Protection and Privacy Act covers collection/processing in Uganda and processing outside Uganda concerning Ugandan citizens. It defines personal data to include identity data and assigned identification numbers. Section 3 requires accountability, fair/lawful processing, adequate/relevant/non-excessive data, lawful retention, data quality, transparency/participation, and security. Sections 7 and 13 require a valid basis and information before collection; consent is one basis, not a blanket substitute for identifying the proper basis. Section 18 limits retention and requires secure deletion/de-identification at expiry. Section 19 addresses processing/storage outside Uganda and requires adequate protection or consent. Section 21 covers safeguards and processor security. Section 23 and regulation 33 address breach notification; regulation 33(1) says notification to the Office is made immediately after occurrence of a data breach. The Act and Regulations include access, correction, objection/prevention, and related subject rights. The Act requires data-protection registration; PDPO's official guidance describes annual registration renewal.

Regulation 22(1) of the Digital Networks Regulations requires recoverable system backup for registration/account data. Regulation 22(2) says a licensee retains customer data for at least five years from collection. The words “customer data” and their application to particular driver applications/evidence, rejected applicants, client bookings, backups, and account records require counsel interpretation. No source reviewed provides a basis to automatically apply a shorter period to all such data or to retain every category indefinitely.

**Product implication:** show field-specific purpose, mandatory/optional status, consequence of not providing it, recipients, retention period, rights/contact, and any legal requirement before collection. National ID number and images must be available only to a narrow, named review group; use encrypted storage, opaque keys, access logging, least privilege, backups, and tested deletion/holds. Do not put ID values/images into logs, analytics, public catalog responses, or photo URLs. Identify every processor, hosting region, and cross-border transfer; do not infer that a vendor's location or certification settles s. 19.

### Payments, tax, incidents, and security

The National Payment Systems Act defines payment services to include executing payment transactions and other services incidental to transferring funds; its licensing regime generally prohibits offering a payment service without a Bank of Uganda licence. Whether Fidexa LLC merely uses a licensed provider or itself performs regulated activity depends on the actual merchant, custody, settlement, refund, and driver-payout flows.

The Digital Networks Regulations call for business, cyber-security, data-recovery, backup and continuity plans as part of licensing materials (reg. 4(3)(b)–(c)); periodic regulatory reporting includes incidents and significant security breaches (reg. 21). Regulation 23 requires a designated regulatory-liaison contact. Data Protection and Privacy Regulations 32–33 address processor safeguards and immediate breach reporting to the Office. NIST SP 800-122 suggests context/risk-based safeguards for PII, including access control, audit/accountability, authentication, media protection, planning, risk assessment and system/communications protection. Those are a useful implementation baseline, not a statutory checklist.

Tax classification depends on who supplies the transport service, who earns a platform fee/commission, whether Fidexa LLC is resident or nonresident for relevant tax purposes, the registration/threshold rules in force, and the provider's status. The VAT Act consolidation page warns that amendments after June 2024 are outstanding, so it is not sufficient by itself to decide the current VAT treatment. URA guidance identifies transport-sector tax and EFRIS issues, but the final treatment and any driver's obligations must be confirmed against current law and the actual contract/payment flow.

**Product implication:** select a licensed payment provider if the legal analysis requires it; disclose merchant/supplier, fees, collection timing, refund route and receipt. Create an incident intake/escalation procedure with a named owner and a route to immediate PDPO notification where required. Confirm URA tax registration, invoicing/EFRIS and withholding obligations for each fee/commission/payout structure with Ugandan tax counsel/accountant; this review does not settle them.

## Issue mapping and release gates

- **#6:** identity capture must support the legal document types applicable to the applicant; present purposes, reviewer categories, legal basis, retention, rights, and current driver service/privacy documents before submission. Do not store an actual ID or accept driver applications until the registration, privacy and retention decisions are approved.
- **#7:** expose only checklist-required evidence to designated, trained reviewers; record actor, timestamp, reason, prior/new status, applicant response, and any exception. Approval is not a substitute for the provider's Competent Authority licence.
- **#8:** collect verified vehicle particulars and evidence; do not show private registration/ownership evidence; publish only approved photos after removing embedded location metadata; enforce valid mandatory licences/inspection/insurance and required marking only after the exact obligations are confirmed. Availability rules prevent overlapping Fave holds but do not certify regulatory eligibility.
- **#10:** disclose the actual supplier and all statutory transaction details before order; let the client review and correct the complete booking; bind quote, trip details, policy versions, explicit contact-sharing choice, and payment evidence to booking. Do not take a deposit while price, payment/custody roles, cancellation, statutory rights, and provider licensing are unresolved.

**No-launch conditions:** no production activation of any of #6/#7/#8/#10 based solely on this draft. Required conditions include counsel's written approval, all regulator/licensing determinations and filings, approved checklist and privacy/terms versions, a lawful retention configuration, operating incident and complaint processes, and the separate phone verification/payment/profile dependencies. Issue #14 itself requires qualified Ugandan counsel review; drafts do not satisfy that acceptance criterion.
