# Uganda MVP payment provider review

**Status:** Provider comparison and provisional recommendation; no provider is selected. The owner confirmed MTN/Airtel mobile money plus local cards for both deposit and final balance on 30 September 2026, reports counsel approved Fave's payment-provider terms on 12 October 2026, and chose a marketplace-style flow on 13 October 2026: clients pay Fave; Fave funds the driver lodging allowance (one flat nightly rate times completed trip nights) from Fave's business funds and pays one night's allowance for each night the driver actually spends on the trip, without a lump-sum advance; the remaining driver amount settles after trip completion and reconciliation. Exact payout timing after each completed night remains open. This per-night direction supersedes the earlier direction for a trip-morning lump-sum advance and unused-night return. The same nightly rate applies across destinations (confirmed 13 October 2026); its amount and evidence remain open. The business-funds source and per-night payout model are the owner's product direction; lawful treatment, operating balance, payout timing, and payout mechanism still need provider/counsel confirmation. Accommodation means the driver's lodging of any kind, not hotels only; Fave does not arrange or pay for client/passenger lodging. This product direction does not itself establish the legal merchant, authorize Fave to hold or transmit funds, or approve a particular provider configuration. Pesapal has confirmed core Uganda online collection support and sent an unsigned merchant sign-up/contract pack, but Fave has not completed provider selection/onboarding, signed a contract, built payment integration, or attempted a real payment.

## Recommendation

Use **Flutterwave as the provisional first choice for merchant onboarding and sandbox validation** for the owner-confirmed MTN/Airtel mobile money and local-card methods, using UGX as the comparison currency and the owner-directed Fave collection, per-night driver lodging payouts, and post-trip driver settlement. Flutterwave must give Fave a written merchant offer confirming eligibility, current fees, payment methods, refunds, settlement, and driver-payout support for per-night lodging payouts and post-trip balances; counsel must confirm the exact merchant/funds-flow configuration is within the terms already approved and identify any additional conditions for #14.

Flutterwave has the clearest publicly verifiable fit for the current product direction: its Uganda documentation describes UGX charges over MTN or Airtel, its current Uganda pricing page lists local cards and mobile-money pricing, and its API documentation covers Uganda bank and mobile-money payouts, sandbox mode, refunds, and marketplace subaccounts. The platform is Hono on Cloudflare Workers, so a server-to-server REST integration can keep secret credentials in Worker secrets; the Expo client should only receive a hosted payment URL or other provider-approved client-safe checkout details.

This is a provisional recommendation, not approval to collect, hold, split, or remit customer funds. Flutterwave’s split-payment documentation places vendor diligence and disputes/chargebacks on the marketplace owner, and Uganda’s payment-law treatment of Fave’s precise funds flow still needs counsel review.

## Funds-flow models to compare

The owner selected the first product model on 13 October 2026: the client pays Fave; Fave funds the driver lodging allowance from its business funds and pays one night's allowance for each night the driver actually spends on the trip, without a lump-sum advance, then settles the remaining driver amount after trip completion and reconciliation. The allowance amount, exact payout timing, and operational/reconciliation controls remain open. The remaining model is shown for comparison only. This does not decide whether Fave is legally the merchant of record or whether the arrangement is regulated; the signed provider offer and counsel's review of the actual implementation must settle those points before live funds are accepted.

### Flutterwave's written Uganda response — 30 September 2026

Flutterwave support (ticket **FLW-539096**, response from Natalie C, Rest of Africa Support) confirmed that Uganda is supported and that the Uganda setup can accept UGX through MTN Mobile Money, Airtel Money, and local cards. Separate deposit and final-balance transactions are supported. This is a screening response, not merchant approval or a signed offer: Fave must submit the Uganda contact-sales form, pass eligibility review, and receive an account link before onboarding.

The response gave **indicative** collection pricing of 3% for mobile money and 4.8% for local and international cards. It did not confirm Fave's VAT/tax treatment, reserves, holds, limits, minimums, or exact fee-bearing setup; sales must confirm those during onboarding. The response said the sales team can advise how to include processing costs in the client-facing fare.

For driver settlement, Flutterwave said Uganda subaccounts can be used by registered businesses processing in UGX, with direct settlement to a verified Ugandan bank account or registered business MTN/Airtel wallet. A platform commission can be configured as a fixed amount or percentage using `split_value`. For the owner-selected flow, Fave must be able to transfer one nightly lodging allowance after each completed trip night and settle the remaining driver amount after completion and reconciliation; Flutterwave identified manual Transfers or the Transfers API for Fave-collected funds. This makes payout timing, funding availability, and account-specific approval essential parts of the written offer. The response described local Uganda settlements as next business day, but did not confirm timing or eligibility for the repeated driver payouts or final settlement under Fave's account, nor settle Fave's regulatory status or the precise responsibility for subaccount disputes and chargebacks.

The response stated refund timelines of 3–15 business days for cards, 5–7 days for mobile money, and 24–48 hours for bank transfers. It also said processing fees are not included in refund amounts, gave chargeback response windows of 24 working hours for local and 48 working hours for international disputes, and cited a USD 38 (or equivalent) international dispute fee. Flutterwave did **not** confirm partial-refund rules or final refund-status reporting for Fave's account; the technical team must confirm those. Treat the stated timelines, fee treatment, and dispute fee as provider-reported screening information until the merchant contract and schedule confirm them.

The provider listed typical Uganda business onboarding documents: incorporation certificate; Form 20/director and beneficial-owner details; TIN; corporate bank details and a bank reference or statement; proof of address under three months old; IDs for directors and shareholders with at least 5% ownership; board resolution; business description; applicable operating licences; and website terms and conditions. Flutterwave said transport-specific licensing requirements would be reviewed by its compliance team. Hosted checkout and a Hono/Cloudflare server-side API integration were described as compatible; the email recommended test keys, server-only credentials, transaction verification, and a unique `tx_ref` for idempotency.

This response strengthens Flutterwave's provisional product-fit case, but it is not the formal merchant offer required to choose a provider. Keep Pesapal's response and unsigned pack as the competing local written evidence; neither provider has confirmed Fave's final merchant/funds-flow structure, account fees, and launch terms.

### Public Flutterwave fee evidence

Flutterwave’s [current Uganda pricing page](https://www.flutterwave.com/ug/pricing) lists 3% for local wallets/mobile-money collection, 4.8% for local card transactions, and 4.8% for international cards. It lists UGX 5,000 per bank transfer payout, UGX 1,000 for mobile-money payouts below UGX 125,000, and 1.2% for payouts from UGX 125,000. It says pricing excludes VAT and other local taxes, and its FAQ says local payments are received the next day. Fees are subject to account/provider terms.

An older [Flutterwave Uganda help article](https://www.flutterwave.com/rw/support/general/heres-all-you-need-to-know-about-operating-a-flutterwave-account-in-uganda) lists 3.2% for local cards and 3.8% for international cards, versus 4.8% on the current pricing page. Treat this as a material pricing discrepancy: obtain a dated written quote for Fave before setting customer prices or the #15 fare model.

For scale illustration only, assume a UGX 100,000 fare split into a 50% deposit and 50% balance, consistent with the proposed #15 defaults. Applying current published rates to each collection installment, before VAT and any driver-payout cost:

| Deposit method | Balance method | Published collection-fee estimate |
|---|---|---:|
| Mobile money (3%) | Mobile money (3%) | UGX 3,000 total |
| Local card (4.8%) | Local card (4.8%) | UGX 4,800 total |
| Mobile money (3%) | Local card (4.8%) | UGX 3,900 total |
| Local card (4.8%) | Mobile money (3%) | UGX 3,900 total |

These are published-list collection-fee calculations, not a Fave offer; the UGX 100,000 value is only a scale example. Flutterwave currently lists UGX 5,000 for a bank payout or UGX 1,000 for a mobile-money payout below UGX 125,000 (1.2% from UGX 125,000), before VAT. Therefore, if the full UGX 100,000 example were paid to a driver in one transfer, a mobile-money payout would add UGX 1,000 or a bank payout UGX 5,000 to the provider-cost illustration. Actual payout costs depend on the amount, payout method, any Fave commission, transfer frequency, and Fave's merchant terms; commission and payout frequency are still undecided. Flutterwave's page says customers bear processing charges by default, with a dashboard option to change who pays. The current working draft in #15 proposes one all-in fare that includes expected provider fees and no undisclosed checkout surcharge. If the owner approves that proposal as final, Fave must configure the merchant account so the client is charged the quoted total, then confirm the effective fee treatment in writing; do not rely on the provider default or add a surprise checkout fee.

## Uganda authorization check

The Bank of Uganda’s [2025 National Payment Systems Oversight Framework](https://bou.or.ug/uploads/Revised_BOU_National_Payment_Systems_Oversight_Framework_2025_698b3e9745.pdf) says PSPs and payment-system operators must be licensed to operate in Uganda. Its eligible activities include domestic money-transfer/payment-gateway services and merchant acquisition. This establishes a compliance check for the actual service and funds flow; it does not establish whether a named provider, contracting affiliate, bank/acquirer, or Fave itself is licensed or exempt.

Before selecting a provider, obtain the contracting entity’s full legal name and written confirmation of the Uganda-authorized entity/partner and its role in each collection, settlement, refund, and payout step. Ask Ugandan counsel to assess the full arrangement, including whether Fave’s merchant, marketplace, collection, or driver-settlement role requires its own authorization or an approved structure. Do not infer authorization from a provider’s ability to accept UGX or from a public product page.
